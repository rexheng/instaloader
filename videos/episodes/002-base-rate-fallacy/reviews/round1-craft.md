## What I saw (flipbook notes)

- **0:00–0:15 cold open.** The first frame is concrete: "1 in 1,000", red "sick" node, blue "healthy" node. The arrows grow as the rates are spoken (99% at 0:05, 1% at 0:09), the "you" ring closes on "yours says positive", and the "?" meter appears with "Pick a number" and a 2.1 s hold. The sync is good. Composition is weak: for 9 s everything sits in the left 45% of the frame and the right half is empty (kf 0:06.1).
- **0:19–0:24.** The red arrow's "99%" flies onto the meter as "naive 99%". It is a good semantic move (the naive rule copies P(+|sick)). For about 0.5 s around 0:21.2 the flying copy is a smeared glyph next to the meter title.
- **0:25–0:43 naive build.** The diagram straightens into the counting table (nodes become row dots, + becomes the column header), which is a strong transform. The grid turns half red, the beam sweeps, the counts tick to 4,950 / 50, and the meter fills to the naive marker (kf 0:41.1). The narration matches the picture within ±1 s throughout.
- **0:43–1:13 fail.** Red dissolves to blue and the counts tick down, the 10 sick get rings, the same beam sweeps, the counts land on 10 / 100, the meter fills 9% red, and a brace reads "11× too high" (1:09). The healthy row pulses on "1% of nearly everyone" (1:13). This is the strongest stretch of the episode.
- **1:15–1:19.** "Ruled out everyone who tested negative": the 9,890 negatives vanish, but the 110 survivors are 5–6 px specks scattered over an empty square (kf 1:18.6). The key "what's left" picture is dust, so the viewer cannot see the crowd that the next sentence names.
- **1:21–2:01 insight.** The 110 fly into a 22×5 strip outlined in yellow, with the two red columns sitting exactly over the 9% red segment of the meter. This picture is excellent. The formula is built in stages from the strip, then the table, then ÷10,000, then the names. Two problems: (a) at 1:29.0 the copy flying from the meter into "≈ 9%" renders as a striped blue blob, because the whole bar is being squashed into a glyph; (b) from 1:31 to 2:01 the frame has three competing focal groups (strip+meter, table, equation), and at 1:57 it carries 13 prose words ("110 people who tested", "chance you're sick", "people", "sick", "healthy", "Bayes' theorem"), over the 12-word limit. The numbers-to-names morph (1:53→1:55) changes all eight factors at once with no per-term flag, and it is the single step a viewer would rewind.
- **2:01–2:24 second test.** The table and formula clear and the strip and meter centre. The meter resets to "?" for a prediction pause (2:11–2:13), the beam dims 99 healthy, and the 11 survivors spread into "11 people who tested + twice" at 91%. The motion is clean. As content it is a near copy of Veritasium's *The Bayesian Trap* (2017), which uses the same 99% test, 0.1% prevalence, 9% answer and independent second test giving 91%.
- **2:25–3:04 payoff.** The mammogram problem is drawn in the opening's grammar (good continuity). The 160 white dots (2:31–2:42) are decoration: they do not become anything. At 2:43 they vanish while the "160 gynecologists" label crossfades through the axis title and the two texts overlap illegibly for about 0.5 s (extracted frame 2:43.2). In the chart the "chose it" values sit about 640 px from the ends of their bars (kf 2:50.8). The shrunken side diagram loses its "cancer"/"no cancer" labels, and its "1 in 100" crowds the "chose it" header. The citation sits about 19 px under the x-axis title (under the 20 px minimum), so the two grey lines read as one block. The bars move from before to after (2:57–3:03) by transform, which is good.
- **3:04.2–3:04.8.** The chart fades to an empty frame (blank run 0.57 s) and the cold-open diagram fades in: a cut at the most important continuity moment, although the payoff diagram has the opening diagram's exact topology.
- **3:05–3:31 landing.** "1 in 1,000" grows to "1 in 100" and the meter swings to 50% and back (an honest limit, made measurable). The arrows become flows whose width is people carried: a hairline red 10 against a fat blue 100 (kf 3:29.6). "99% is how often the sick test positive / 9% is how often the positive are sick" pulses the right objects. The episode ends 1.7 s after the last word on a plain series card. The ending is excellent and re-sees the first frame exactly.

## Audio / sync

"scan scan" at 2:37 has overlapping word timestamps (157.07–157.71 and 157.45–157.67), which points to a Whisper artifact rather than a real stutter, so I made no deduction. Cadence: about 65 of the 70 internal pauses are 0.4–0.6 s and sit at every sentence boundary, while the within-phrase rate is 185 wpm. That is a metronomic TTS rhythm: fast phrases separated by identical gaps, with no lingering on the reveals ("That's nine percent" at 1:03, "eleven times too high" at 1:06). Score 8, not 9.

## FIXES (highest impact first)

1. **[0:00–0:15, 2:01–2:24][Originality/Hook] STRUCTURAL.** The hook numbers (99% test, 1 in 1,000, 9%) and the second-test beat (independent retest gives 91%) are the exact skeleton of Veritasium's *The Bayesian Trap* (2017). A viewer who has seen it already knows both answers, and Originality caps the whole episode through the ceiling rule. Make two changes. (a) Make the viewer compete with real doctors in the cold open: keep the 99% diagram and add, at 0:10, a ghost of the gynecologists' "90%" bar and the line "Most doctors given this kind of problem got it wrong. Pick a number." This puts the payoff's data (47% said 90%) up front as the stakes and has the viewer commit against a measured human baseline. (b) Delete the second-test beat (2:01–2:24, about 23 s) and replace it with a data-science consequence in the same strip-and-meter picture, for example: "A fraud model that is 99% accurate, at 1 fraud in 1,000 transactions: of the alerts it raises, 9% are fraud. Data scientists call this share precision." Relabel the meter "precision" for 3 s. This keeps the runtime and gives the series its DS angle. It is the only route to Originality ≥ 8.
2. **[1:15–1:20][Visual/Clarity]** When the negatives vanish, the 110 survivors are 5–6 px specks (kf 1:18.6). In the same animation as the FadeOut of negatives, scale each survivor dot ×4 (radius to ≥ 0.06 units) and keep the 10 sick ringed. Then fly them to the strip. "What's left" must be visibly a crowd.
3. **[3:04.2–3:05][Motion]** Replace the fade to an empty frame (0.57 s blank) with a transform. FadeOut the chart and citation, move the mini mammogram diagram to the cold-open position, and TransformMatchingTex "1 in 100" to "1 in 1,000", "90%" to "99%" and "9%" to "1%" (2 s), with "no cancer"/"cancer" morphing to "healthy"/"sick". The viewer then sees that the doctors' problem and theirs are the same picture.
4. **[1:49–1:56][Clarity/Motion]** The numbers-to-names step morphs all factors at once. Do it in two beats with a ≤ 1 s colour flag on each: first 0.001→P(sick) and 0.999→P(healthy) (pulse "1 in 1,000" on screen if visible), then 0.99→P(+|sick) and 0.01→P(+|healthy) (pulse the table's 99% and 1% arrows). Hold the numeric form ≥ 6 s before morphing (currently about 5 s for about 10 terms).
