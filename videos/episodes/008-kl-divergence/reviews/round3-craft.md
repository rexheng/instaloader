## Notes behind the scores

- **Hook 8.5.** The data is on the first frame. The wrong belief ("if closeness works both ways, it stays at one") is voiced, and the viewer commits to 1, 2 or 3 by 0:12.5 with a 1.96 s hold, and each label lands within 0.2 s of its spoken number. Tension rises through ×2.2, the 5.77 mountain and the second question. Two things hold it back. The answer is shown for only about 1.5 s at 2:32 and is not drawn on the opening ghosts (fix 2). And the bet asks about "putting the bell first" before the viewer can reason about what that means, so the commitment is closer to a guess than a held misconception.
- **Clarity 8.5.** Concrete comes before abstract throughout: one length, then the doublings, then every length, then the weight, the integral and the refunds. Every number I recomputed matches to two decimals. Assumptions and limits are stated, including the raw-points footnote and both smoothings. The deductions: the transient false equation during the swap (fix 1), and the payoff's 6-second leap from "data" to "posterior, same curve, as a stand-in" (2:41–2:47), which a first-time viewer will need to rewatch.
- **Visual 8.5.** The palette is semantic and strictly held (blue data, purple bell, red bits, yellow focus, grey refunds and citations), the type is large and passes §5, and I found no overlaps at rest. Focal points are clear in most frames (kf_14, kf_20, kf_23). The deductions: the crowded top-left corner in the payoff, the undifferentiated six-line formula column, the colliding baselines and missing leader in the honest limit, the empty 30 % panel for 12 s, and A drawn in its de-emphasised style while it is the subject of the landing.
- **Motion 8.5.** Objects persist and transform with meaning: the argument arcs, the bar pull-outs, q stacking, the scanner, the fill integrating left to right, both bills changing live along the descent path, and the brackets flying into (0.44/1.14)². The deductions: the swap's step order (fix 1), the 2:33 teardown that dims everything and morphs stale axes across the chart (fix 3), the pip-sized flashes at 1:52–1:55, and the overlap of "?" and the number at 1:59.
- **Audio 9.** Pace is 164 wpm over speech, loudness −14.0 LUFS and true peak −1.6 dBTP. The deictics land: "this one" with bell A at 0:05, "one / two / three" with their labels, "short ones like this" with the 36 % box at 1:41, "in the gap … tails" with the boxes at 2:09–2:13, and "three" with the yellow 3 at 2:31.8. Pauses are placed at beat turns. The proper names render acceptably. I cannot show an odd emphasis from the evidence, so I deduct nothing.
- **Concision 8.5.** About 5 % could go. The honest-limit beat spends 18 s on two smoothings. "Every p trades places with q" and "the bell supplies the lengths, and the data judges them" explain the same swap twice (both are good; one could be shorter).
- **Originality 9.** The two side-by-side bills, one draining while the other swells, take the textbook mode-covering vs mode-seeking figure (Bishop Fig. 10.3) somewhere new. With the doublings stack and real Old Faithful data, this episode has an image viewers would remember.
