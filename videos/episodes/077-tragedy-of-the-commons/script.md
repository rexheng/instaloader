# 077 — The Tragedy of the Commons Isn't Inevitable

**One idea:** a herder overgrazes because he keeps the whole gain of a cow but feels only one-Nth of its damage; a counted cap with a fine hands him back the other N−1 slices, which is how Törbel has kept a shared pasture green for five centuries.

## Palette (one meaning each)
| Colour | Meaning |
|---|---|
| GREEN | grass: the pasture fill, "grass left" |
| BLUE | milk: the village-milk curve, a cow's gain |
| RED | damage: milk the herd loses to one extra cow |
| YELLOW | what one herder personally bears: his cows, his slice, the fine |
| PURPLE | the "saints" (herders who never add cows) |
| INK / GRAY | text, axes, neutral cows |

## Beats (narration exactly as spoken)

**0 · Cold open (0:00)** — Pasture disc, cows, "Törbel, Swiss Alps".
> This pasture above Törbel, in the Swiss Alps, has been shared by a whole village for more than five hundred years. A famous argument says it should be bare dirt by now. What kept it green? Make a guess. [pause 1.5]

A grey ring sweeps once round the pasture during the pause (the thinking time).

**1 · Stakes** — disc slides left, Hardin quote right, citation.
> In nineteen sixty eight, Garrett Hardin argued in Science that anything shared, from pastures to fisheries, gets used up. Freedom in a commons, he wrote, brings ruin to all.

**2 · Naive build** — quote yields to the milk-per-cow rule and a chart.
> So build his pasture. Ten herders, five cows each. On fresh grass a cow gives twenty litres of milk a day, but cows eat grass, so every added cow cuts every cow's milk by a fifth of a litre.

**3** — the curve draws; dot rides to the peak.
> Plot the whole village's milk against the size of the herd. It peaks right here: fifty cows, five hundred litres a day.

**4 · Fail** — herd grows 50 → 90, grass fades, dot slides down.
> Now let each herder add a cow whenever it pays him. They stop at ninety cows. The grass is down to a tenth, and the village gets a hundred and eighty litres instead of five hundred.

**5 · Fix 1: goodwill** — reset, three saints in purple, others refill.
> If you guessed trust, try it. Make three herders saints who never go past five cows. The other seven fill the gap, and the herd settles at eighty nine. The saints just earn half as much milk.

**6 · Insight** — chart leaves; one herder's five cows turn yellow; bars.
> Why does nobody stop? Rewind to fifty cows and watch this herder add one more.

**7**
> The new cow gives him nine point eight litres. It also costs each of the fifty cows a fifth of a litre, ten litres of damage. For the village, this cow is a loss.

**8** — red bar splits into ten slices; one slides to him; formula built from bars.
> But the damage is shared by ten herds, and only this one slice lands on him. One litre. So he adds the cow whenever the gain beats the damage over N.

**9 · Prediction pause**
> How big must the damage get before he stops? [pause 1.2] Ten times the gain. At ninety cows, the next one gives one point eight litres and does eighteen litres of damage.

**10 · Fix 2** — back to fifty; the nine other slices turn yellow and stack onto his.
> So hand him back the other nine slices. Fine every cow over five by nine litres. Now his rule is gain against the whole damage, and the fifty first cow costs him money.

**11** — chart returns; herders re-run with the fine.
> Run the herders again. They hold at fifty, the grass at half, and the village milk is back to five hundred.

**12 · Payoff 1** — Törbel's rule quoted; citations.
> That is Törbel's rule. No one may send more cows to the alp than he can feed through the winter, and an elected official fines anyone who does. Elinor Ostrom built Governing the Commons on cases like this, and in two thousand nine became the first woman to win the Nobel Prize in economics.

**13 · Payoff 2** — two bars, real data.
> Will ordinary people pay to fine a neighbour? Fehr and Gächter gave groups of four a shared pot. By the last round, without fines, people put in about three tokens out of twenty. With fines allowed, about eighteen.

**14 · Honest limit**
> A fine only works if someone counts. Törbel counts every cow the day the herd goes up the mountain. Where nobody can count, Hardin's arithmetic comes back.

**15 · Landing** — the opening pasture, centred again, the nine slices drawn round it.
> So the guess that trust kept Törbel green is the one the model rules out. What keeps it green is a counted herd, and a fine that hands each herder back the nine slices the commons takes away.
