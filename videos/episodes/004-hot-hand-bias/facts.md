## Simulated vs real on screen

- The 16 sequences and their scores are exhaustive enumeration (exact, not sampled).
- Round 6: the simulated 100-shot record was removed; nothing simulated is shown.
- The ghost ticks for 1 and 2 hits in a row and their 1,000-shot positions are exact expectations (dynamic programming), shown as positions only.
- The −8 point baseline is Miller & Sanjurjo's computed value (cited). The 46% / 54% marker positions are exact expectations (dynamic programming, no sampling), the curves of Miller & Sanjurjo's Fig. 1. No simulated value is shown on screen (the v1 "1,000 shots" ghost was removed).
- 49 % / 45 % / +4 are real (GVT's Cornell data as reproduced by Miller & Sanjurjo, Table 2).

## Things deliberately not claimed

- Not "the hot hand is proven real": only "evidence for a hot hand, not proof".
- Not "the coin has memory": every single flip stays 50/50; the bias comes from averaging per-sequence proportions.
- Not the in-game 76ers or Celtics results (confounded by defense / shot selection; MS fn. 22–23).
- Not "this one 4-flip row is hot": the closing line is "A fifty percent shooter who averages one half on this test is already running hot" (an average over many rows, as the 17/42 baseline is). Round 2: the picture now matches. HHTH's own 1/2 stays where it is; the ghost of all sixteen rows collapses onto 17/42 (the cold-open question answered), and the shooter is a separate blue dot on the one-half line, with the "hot" arrow drawn from 17/42 to it.
- Not "the hot hand survives defense": the honest limit names the gap between a gym and a game (row 20).
