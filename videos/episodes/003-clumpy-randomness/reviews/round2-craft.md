## Notes behind the scores

- **Hook 8.5.** The first frame is the puzzle: dots raining into two maps with live "537 hits" counters (0:00–0:07). "Which one is chance?" lands at 0:08.7, followed by a 2.0 s hold, and the WWII stakes plus a cited quote arrive by 0:25. The landing resolves the opening exactly ("If you picked map B, you picked the one with a plan", 3:46–3:50). Two things hold it below 9. The random-vs-even dot pair is a familiar demonstration, and tension sags through the 47 s algebra stretch (1:37–2:24).
- **Clarity 8.5.** Concrete first, every number checks, and the derivation is now built from the picture: λ from the 537 counter, P(0) from the square, the 537-bomb row, two bombs dropping into the enlarged square. Deductions: the hidden 537·536 ≈ 537² step (2:07), the invisible river (3:34), and a five-step derivation in 47 s that a first-time viewer could follow but probably not re-derive.
- **Visual 8.** The palette is semantic and stable (blue = observed, purple = chance, red = flagged/aim, yellow = focus), with safe margins, no text overlaps at rest and readable type (citations about 26 px cap height). Several minor issues add up: two focal points with olive dimming during the derivation (1:38–2:23), the cluttered Clarke frame (3:09–3:24), insets covering data (0:51–1:00), λ^k in the wrong colour (2:17), gray text at 6.2:1, and a real map with no geography.
- **Motion 8.** Persistence is the episode's strength. The grid sweep, lagged outlines with a live counter, TransformFromCopy of 1/576, 537 map dots flying into the bomb row (1:53–1:55), cells flying into bars (2:29), A's bars morphing into B's (2:42) and the real map raining in all carry meaning. The resting statics are all under 4 s. The shredded P(2)→P(k) morph (2:07.5), the "false alarms" letter scramble (0:49.2) and the P(empty)/P(0) ghost (1:52.7) are exactly the moments the algebra or the punchline should read cleanly.
- **Audio 8.** Loudness, peak and lead/trail silence are on target. Deictics land: "like this one" with the red square at 0:34.9, "these purple bars" as they grow at 2:21–2:23, "half the expected empty squares" as bar 0 flashes yellow at 2:43, and the arrows within about 0.5 s of "extra empty squares" (2:55.0) and "extra pile-ups" (2:56.5). "Poisson" is pronounced correctly. Deductions: the unseen river, a uniform 0.4–0.5 s sentence cadence, top-of-band pace through the derivation, and probable mis-stress at 2:49 and 2:52.
- **Concision 8.5.** Little is removable. "Four in ten, with almost a bomb per square" (1:20.5) half-repeats the line before it. At 3:51.6 the episode is near the runtime ceiling, but each beat is doing work.
- **Originality 8.5.** Clarke's flying bombs is a textbook example. Framing it as "the detector your eye is running", inverting it with "too even is the fingerprint", and adding a real digitised V-1 map with a density-gradient limit make it fresh and specific. There is no slop.
